Add Kelviq as a payment method in your [WooCommerce](https://woocommerce.com) store. Shoppers fill their cart as usual, pay on a secure Kelviq checkout, and come back to your store. Kelviq is the Merchant of Record: it takes the payment, charges the right sales tax or VAT for the shopper's country, and sends the invoice.

<Warning>
  Kelviq for WooCommerce is for digital products, software and subscriptions. Don't use it to sell physical goods.
</Warning>

## How it works

1. The shopper places an order and chooses the Kelviq payment method.
2. They're taken to Kelviq checkout to pay the order total, with cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and the other [payment methods](/checkout/payment-methods) you've turned on.
3. Kelviq tells your store the payment succeeded, and the shopper returns to the order confirmation page. Before marking the order paid, the plugin checks the payment with the Kelviq API.

Coupons are applied in WooCommerce, so the shopper pays the discounted total. Kelviq discount codes are turned off for these checkouts.

## Before you start

You need:

- A WordPress site with WooCommerce 8.0 or later, reachable from the internet over **HTTPS**. Kelviq sends payment confirmations to your site, so it can't be a local or password-protected site.
- A Kelviq **Server API key**, from **Settings → API keys**.
- A **one-time plan** that WooCommerce orders are sold under:
  1. Open **Products** and add a product, for example `WooCommerce store`.
  2. Add a plan with a one-time price, for example `WooCommerce order`, and publish it. The price doesn't matter: the shopper always pays the order total.
  3. Copy the plan's **identifier**, for example `woocommerce-order`.

<Tip>
  Set up everything in [sandbox mode](/guides/sandbox) first, with a sandbox API key and plan. You can then pay with [test cards](/guides/test-payments) before taking real payments.
</Tip>

## Install the plugin

1. In Kelviq, open **Settings → Integrations**, click **Configure WooCommerce**, then **Download plugin**.
2. In WordPress, open **Plugins → Add New Plugin → Upload Plugin**.
3. Choose `kelviq-for-woocommerce.zip`, click **Install Now**, then **Activate**.

## Connect it to Kelviq

1. In WordPress, open **WooCommerce → Settings → Payments**, and click **Kelviq**.
2. Tick **Enable Kelviq**.
3. Choose the **Environment**: **Sandbox** for test payments or **Live** for real ones.
4. Paste your **Server API key** for that environment.
5. Enter the **Kelviq plan identifier**.
6. Choose how **Tax** works:
   - **My prices include tax** (default): the shopper pays exactly your price, and Kelviq takes the tax out of it.
   - **Add tax on top of my prices**: Kelviq adds tax for the shopper's country at checkout.
7. Optionally, change the **Title** and **Description** shoppers see at checkout.
8. Click **Save changes**.

The plugin checks your key and plan, then creates a webhook endpoint in Kelviq for your store, named `WooCommerce: <your site>`. You'll see **Kelviq is connected** at the top of the page.

<Note>
  Kelviq calculates tax, so turn off WooCommerce taxes in **WooCommerce → Settings → General** (untick **Enable tax rates and calculations**). Otherwise shoppers are charged tax twice.
</Note>

## Refunds

- **From WooCommerce:** open the order, click **Refund**, enter the amount, and click the **Refund … via** button with your Kelviq title. The refund is made in Kelviq.
- **From Kelviq:** refund the order in **Sales → Orders**. The refund also appears on the WooCommerce order. A full refund sets the order to **Refunded**.

## Order details

Each paid order shows:

- The Kelviq order ID as the **Transaction ID**.
- Order notes for each step, for example *Shopper sent to Kelviq checkout* and *Paid with Kelviq*.

In Kelviq, each checkout has the WooCommerce order ID and number in its [metadata](/checkout/checkout-metadata) (`wc_order_id`, `wc_order_number`), so you can match orders in both places.

## Troubleshooting

Errors are shown at the top of the Kelviq settings page, and on the order as order notes. For more detail, tick **Debug log** and open **WooCommerce → Status → Logs** (source `kelviq`).

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Kelviq did not accept the API key | Paste a Server API key from **Settings → API keys** for the environment you chose. A sandbox key doesn't work in Live, and the other way around. |
| No plan "…" in this environment | Check the plan identifier in **Products**, and that the plan is published in the same environment as your key. |
| Could not set up payment confirmations | Your site isn't reachable from the internet over HTTPS, for example it's a local site or behind a password. Put the site online, then click **Save changes** again. |
| Kelviq doesn't show at checkout | Tick **Enable Kelviq**, and fill in the API key and plan identifier. |
| We could not start the payment | The order note says why, for example a currency Kelviq doesn't support. |
| Order stays "Pending payment" after paying | Open **Settings → Webhooks** in Kelviq, find `WooCommerce: <your site>`, and check its deliveries. Resend a failed one. The order is also confirmed when the shopper returns to your store. |

## Disconnect

Untick **Enable Kelviq** and save to stop offering it at checkout. When you delete the plugin, it also deletes its webhook endpoint in Kelviq.
